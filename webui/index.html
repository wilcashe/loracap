2. Radio

The band decides which spreading factors (SF) exist on uplink. The chip fills in sensitivity, channels and the demodulation limit from its datasheet.

This plan publishes a different payload ceiling for each setting, and at the slow data rates they differ by more than tenfold, so there is nothing safe to assume on your behalf.

3. Propagation

The one thing that cannot be borrowed from another site. Everything downstream rests on it, so how you arrived at it is recorded in study.py alongside the numbers.

Propagation model* from field measurements, or from the environment

A CSV (comma-separated values) file of received messages with the received signal strength (RSSI), latitude and longitude. Filter it first to one spreading factor and one gateway.

Published measurements give the exponent for a comparable environment. They do not give the intercept, and free-space loss is not a usable stand-in: on the one deployment here with field data it is about 59 dB optimistic at 100 m, because a node on an animal radiates through a body, close to wet ground and often through vegetation. So the intercept is anchored to the one thing you can state without a campaign, which is how far the link is expected to work.

sets the exponent and derives the intercept
Shadowing σ. The spread the mean trend does not explain, drawn once per node and fixed: obstruction that belongs to where the node is. Leaving it at 0 makes two nodes the same distance away receive identical power, which no site does. Adopting a fit fills it from that fit's residual.

4. Traffic and experiment

Capacity is the node count at which the data extraction rate (DER), the share of transmitted packets the gateway receives, falls below the threshold. Use the shortest reporting interval the deployment can produce: capacity measured at the worst case is a bound you can defend.

Scenarios* at least one
  • . Every node on that one spreading factor.
  • MULTI. Every node on the fastest spreading factor its link reaches: ideal, instantly converged assignment with no margin and full power, so it is a bound rather than a network.
  • ADR (adaptive data rate). What a converged real network settles into, keeping a link margin and reducing transmit power.
  • Running MULTI and ADR together shows what the idealisation is worth.

5. Create and run

Writing the study puts study.py and site_polygon.py in studies/<name>/. From there it is an ordinary study, editable by hand.

Checks

Updated as you type. A bad parameter does not make the simulation fail, it makes it return a wrong answer quietly. Click a message to go to its field.

Check messages come from the simulator and are shown in English. Each one names the field it concerns and leads to it with a click.

Figures

Recorded in study.py with the rest of the study, so run.py plot redraws exactly what this page drew. Nothing here changes a number, and the defaults draw the figure as it has always been drawn.

Which figures
always

Packets transmitted times DER, scaled to an hour, against the traffic offered. A circle marks each scenario's capacity.
Transmit energy per message that arrives, from the node radio's current table. Sleep current is not in it, so it is not a battery life.
