2. Radio

The band decides which spreading factors exist on uplink. The chip fills in sensitivity, channels and the demodulation limit from its datasheet.

This plan publishes a different payload ceiling for each setting, and at the slow data rates they differ by more than tenfold, so there is nothing safe to assume on your behalf.

3. Propagation not set

The one thing that cannot be borrowed from another site. Everything downstream rests on it, so how you arrived at it is recorded in study.py alongside the numbers.

A CSV of received messages with RSSI, latitude and longitude. Filter it first to one spreading factor and one gateway.

none

Published measurements give the exponent for a comparable environment. They do not give the intercept, and free-space loss is not a usable stand-in: on the one deployment here with field data it is about 59 dB optimistic at 100 m, because a node on an animal radiates through a body, close to wet ground and often through vegetation. So the intercept is anchored to the one thing you can state without a campaign, which is how far the link is expected to work.

sets the exponent and derives the intercept
Shadowing is the spread the mean trend does not explain, drawn once per node and fixed: obstruction that belongs to where the node is. Leaving it at 0 makes two nodes the same distance away receive identical power, which no site does. Adopting a fit fills it from that fit's residual.

4. Traffic and experiment

Use the shortest reporting interval the deployment can produce: capacity measured at the worst case is a bound you can defend.

MULTI gives every node the fastest spreading factor its link reaches: ideal, instantly converged assignment with no margin and full power, so it is a bound rather than a network. ADR is what a converged real network settles into, keeping a link margin and reducing transmit power. Running both shows what the idealisation is worth.

5. Create and run

Writing the study puts study.py and site_polygon.py in studies/<name>/. From there it is an ordinary study, editable by hand.

Checks waiting

Updated as you type. A bad parameter does not make the simulation fail, it makes it return a wrong answer quietly.

Everything here goes through the same engine as python3 run.py. Values and sources are recorded in the generated study.py, and results carry their configuration in results.json.
